# Making episodes with Claude + Narrator Studio

Narrator Studio reads anything aloud, but it shines when the text is written
*for listening*. This guide shows how to get Claude to write scripts the app
understands: who speaks, where the pauses go, when the music plays.

## Text or PDF?

| You have… | Do this |
|---|---|
| An idea or topic | Use a prompt from the **Write** tab, paste Claude's reply into the app (**Paste script**). Plain text keeps the speakers, pauses and music cues. |
| A PDF / article / notes you want *turned into* an episode | Attach the PDF to one of the prompts in Claude. Claude rewrites it for the ear in your chosen style. |
| A book or paper you just want read | Open the `.pdf` or `.txt` directly (**Add → Open a file**). It reads as an audiobook; the app removes page numbers, running headers and hyphenation. |

Rule of thumb: **ask Claude for plain text, not a PDF.** A PDF loses the
speaker tags. If Claude offers a file, a `.txt` or `.md` file is perfect too
(share it to Narrator Studio, or open it from Files).

## The workflow

1. In the app, open **Write**, type your topic, pick a length, tap **Copy prompt** on a style.
2. Open Claude, paste. Attach a PDF if you want it adapted. Send.
3. Copy Claude's whole reply. In the app tap **Paste script** (or share the reply to Narrator Studio).
4. Check the cast with **Voices & style** (the masks icon), press play.
5. Tap **Export** for an M4A you can upload to Spotify for Podcasters, Apple Podcasts, YouTube, etc.

150 words ≈ 1 minute of listening, so a 10-minute episode is about 1,500 words.

## The script format

```text
---
title: Why Is the Sky Blue?
style: podcast
music: curious
voices:
  HOST: Radio Host
  CO-HOST: Co-Host
  GUEST: The Professor
---

HOST: Welcome back to Curious Minds!
CO-HOST: Today's question sounds simple.

## Part 1: Sunlight is a mixture
HOST: So where does the blue come from?
[pause 1]
GUEST: It's already in the sunlight...

[sound: chime]
[music: outro]
HOST: That's all for today.
```

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `--- … ---` header | Optional. `title`, `style`, `music`, and `voices` (speaker tag → character or voice name). |
| `HOST: text` | Spoken by HOST. Tags are CAPITALS (or `**Bold:**`). A paragraph without a tag is the narrator (or continues the previous speaker in a dialogue). |
| `## Part 2` | Section. Announced in audiobook, lecture, story and briefing styles; in podcast/interview it plays a transition sound instead. |
| `[pause 2]` | Silence in seconds (`[pause 500ms]` works too). |
| `[sound: chime]` | `chime`, `ding`, `whoosh` or `pop`. |
| `[music: intro]` / `[music: outro]` / `[music: stop]` | Control the music bed. With music on, an intro and outro are added automatically. |
| `[laughs]`, `(Music fades.)` | Stage directions: skipped, never read. |
| `<!-- note -->` | A note to yourself, never read. |

## Styles

| `style:` | What it sounds like | Default voices | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| `audiobook` | One narrator, chapter titles read aloud, unhurried | The Narrator | off |
| `podcast` | Two hosts with banter, intro + outro music | Radio Host, Co-Host, GUEST = The Professor | Curious Minds |
| `lecture` | A professor, slow, pauses to think, a student asks questions | The Professor, The Student | off |
| `interview` | Host interviews a guest (great for "inspired by" historical figures) | Radio Host, GUEST = The Old Sage | Fireside |
| `story` | Storyteller plus character voices for dialogue | The Storyteller | Deep Space |
| `news` | Crisp briefing, headlines read, chime between stories | News Anchor, Field Reporter | Newsroom |

Music themes: `curious`, `cosmic`, `fireside`, `newsroom`, or `off`. The music
is generated by the app itself, so it is free of any license.

## Famous people (Einstein and friends)

The app does not ship clones of real famous people: their voices and
likenesses are controlled by their estates, and podcast platforms remove
realistic impersonations. What works well, and is honest with listeners:

* Use the **Interview with a great mind** prompt. Claude writes a guest
  *inspired by* the thinker, in the spirit of their real writings, and the
  host says up front that it is a dramatization.
* Cast that guest as **The Old Sage**, **The Professor** or a Voice Gallery
  voice, or mix a voice (e.g. 60% George + 40% Onyx) for an older, wiser sound.
* When you publish, say the voices are AI-generated.

## Prompts

The **Write** tab copies these with your topic and length filled in, plus the
format rules Claude needs. The styles:

* **Two-host podcast**: hook in the first 20 seconds, real back-and-forth, 3–5 parts with an "aha" each, recap and teaser.
* **Interview with a great mind**: a clearly labelled dramatization of a famous thinker explaining their ideas.
* **Professor's lecture**: intuition first, one example per idea, "pause and think" moments, summary.
* **Audiobook chapter**: popular-science prose for one narrator; best for adapting a PDF.
* **Story that teaches**: characters discover the idea through an adventure (kids, bedtime).
* **Daily briefing**: "the five things to know about…", crisp and factual.

If you write your own request, add the format rules: the **Script format**
screen (Write tab → ?) has a button that copies them.

## Writing tips for the ear

* Short sentences, contractions, signposts ("Here's the surprising part…").
* Spell out how numbers and symbols are said ("nineteen oh five", "about three hundred thousand kilometres per second").
* No tables, bullet lists, URLs, citations or footnotes.
* Give each speaker a distinct personality; let them react to each other.
